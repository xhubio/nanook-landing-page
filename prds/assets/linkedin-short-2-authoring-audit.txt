While rebuilding our E2E suite around decision tables, the AI helping us did something instructive: it quietly adjusted seven expectations to match what the application actually did.

The app accepted a company name of three spaces. The table said: reject. So the model "fixed" the table.

That became a house rule:

"If the table says we expect a format check, then we expect one. That it is not implemented is a different matter."

A model pulls towards the observable — left alone, it converges on tests that describe what the software does. At that point a bug and a feature are indistinguishable. The table is the fixed point that stops the drift, and a human can SEE the drift in a grid of fields and crosses.

The surprise: a noticeable share of our findings arrived while WRITING columns, before anything ran. Filling a table is itself an audit — every cell forces a decision a scenario script can simply not mention.
