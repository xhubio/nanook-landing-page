=== Comment 1 — Torsten Link (author; first comment under the post, within the first hour) ===

The step most people skip: counter-probes. A green test proves nothing if it would also be green when the thing does not work. We broke seven safeguards on purpose and counted which tests turned red. Two lessons: expect a number, not "something turns red" (the time zone probe: exactly 3 red). And place the probe where the test reads. On the first try it was in the wrong place, the test stayed green, and the safeguard looked useless. It was not.

=== Comment 2 — Patrick Jerominek (BeeBack UG, the company behind Nanook) ===

The rule that carries the whole approach: if you want to change an expectation, change the table first and write down why. Without this rule, the agent would have had a green suite on day two that checked nothing. That is exactly why the table is the point of truth: it is the place where a person sees a change and has to give a reason. Start here: https://nanook.xhub.io/docs/quickstart/claude-code
